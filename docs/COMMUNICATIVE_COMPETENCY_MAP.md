# Purpose

This document maps the Spanish A0 curriculum to communicative competencies.

It is not a lesson plan and does not define content schemas.

It answers one question:

What can the learner do with the language after each module?

---

# Module 1 — First Words and Reading

## Communicative Competencies

- Greet and say goodbye.
- Use basic courtesy words.
- Ask for repetition or slower speech.
- Recognize the first high-frequency Spanish words in writing.

## Typical Real-Life Scenarios

- entering or leaving a lesson;
- thanking someone;
- asking a speaker to repeat;
- recognizing `hola`, `adiós` and early courtesy phrases.

## Language Patterns Introduced

- fixed greeting and goodbye expressions;
- `por favor`, `gracias`, `perdón`, `de nada`;
- `no entiendo`, `repite`, `más despacio`;
- silent `h` and stable vowel reading support.

## Typical Learner Limitations

- Cannot yet introduce themself beyond isolated words.
- Cannot sustain a conversation.
- Relies on fixed expressions and very short repair phrases.

# Module 2 — Names and Introductions

## Communicative Competencies

- Say one's name.
- Ask another person's name.
- Respond politely in a first meeting.
- Recognize names with early Spanish spelling patterns.

## Typical Real-Life Scenarios

- meeting a classmate;
- introducing oneself to a tutor;
- asking a new person's name;
- completing a short `mucho gusto` exchange.

## Language Patterns Introduced

- `me llamo ...`;
- `¿Cómo te llamas?`;
- `mucho gusto`, `igualmente`;
- early `ll`, `ñ` and `j` recognition in names and familiar words.

## Typical Learner Limitations

- May overuse `soy` for name introduction.
- Cannot yet describe origin or personal details.
- Needs controlled name pools and short exchanges.

---

# Module 3 — Origin and Places

## Communicative Competencies

- Say where one is from.
- Ask where another person is from.
- Understand a tiny profile with name and origin.
- Recognize direct-question accents in familiar questions.

## Typical Real-Life Scenarios

- giving origin in a first conversation;
- asking a new person where they are from;
- reading a short profile;
- recognizing city and country names.

## Language Patterns Introduced

- `soy de ...`;
- `¿De dónde eres?`;
- `es de ...`;
- `qué`, `cómo`, `dónde` in direct questions.

## Typical Learner Limitations

- Cannot yet describe nationality in detail.
- Cannot handle open-ended geography talk.
- Needs controlled place names and short question-answer patterns.

---

# Module 4 — How People Are and Classroom Survival

## Communicative Competencies

- Ask how someone is.
- Give a short wellbeing answer.
- Say whether they speak Spanish a little.
- Ask basic classroom repair questions.

## Typical Real-Life Scenarios

- opening a small conversation;
- answering `¿Qué tal?`;
- saying `un poco`;
- asking what a word means;
- repairing a tutoring or classroom exchange.

## Language Patterns Introduced

- `¿Cómo estás?`, `¿Qué tal?`;
- `bien`, `muy bien`, `mal`, `más o menos`;
- `¿Hablas español?`, `sí`, `no`, `un poco`;
- `¿Qué significa ...?`.

## Typical Learner Limitations

- Cannot yet explain feelings beyond a few fixed responses.
- Cannot manage fast classroom speech.
- Needs short, predictable repair language.

# Module 5 — Numbers and Personal Facts

## Communicative Competencies

- Recognize and produce numbers 0-20.
- Understand short number sequences.
- State a controlled age.
- Use simple quantities with familiar content.

## Typical Real-Life Scenarios

- hearing or giving a short number sequence;
- saying an age in a controlled prompt;
- understanding a simple personal fact;
- using one or two familiar quantities.

## Language Patterns Introduced

- numbers 0-20;
- `número`;
- `tengo ... años`;
- simple quantity patterns with familiar nouns.

## Typical Learner Limitations

- Cannot yet handle prices, dates or time broadly.
- Cannot give complex personal information.
- Needs numbers in short, constrained contexts.

---

# Module 6 — Everyday Objects and Tener

## Communicative Competencies

- Name common everyday objects.
- Use `un/una` with familiar objects.
- Say that someone has a familiar object.
- Understand a simple object-location exchange.

## Typical Real-Life Scenarios

- saying `tengo un libro`;
- asking if someone has a phone or key;
- understanding `aquí` and `allí` with familiar objects;
- correcting short word-order problems.

## Language Patterns Introduced

- object nouns such as book, phone, key, bag, table and chair;
- `un`, `una`;
- `tengo`, `tienes`, `tiene`;
- `está aquí`, `está allí` in controlled use.

## Typical Learner Limitations

- Cannot yet describe objects freely.
- Cannot handle broad article or gender explanations.
- Needs small object pools and explicit person constraints.

---

# Module 7 — Food, Drink and Needs

## Communicative Competencies

- Request basic food or drink politely.
- Say simple hunger, thirst or help needs.
- Complete a tiny cafe-style exchange.
- Reuse courtesy and clarification in a request context.

## Typical Real-Life Scenarios

- asking for water, coffee, bread or cheese;
- saying `por favor` and `gracias`;
- saying `tengo hambre` or `tengo sed`;
- asking for help in a controlled situation.

## Language Patterns Introduced

- food and drink nouns;
- `quiero ...`;
- `tengo hambre`, `tengo sed`;
- `necesito ayuda`;
- polite request exchanges.

## Typical Learner Limitations

- Cannot yet order from a full menu.
- Cannot manage prices or substitutions unless separately taught.
- Cannot describe medical or emergency situations beyond tightly controlled needs.

---

# Module 8 — People, Home and Simple Surroundings

## Communicative Competencies

- State tiny facts about family or a friend.
- Say a simple home or city fact.
- Understand third-person mini-profiles.
- Combine people, places and possession patterns.

## Typical Real-Life Scenarios

- saying `mi amiga ...` or `mi familia ...` in a controlled sentence;
- reading a tiny profile;
- recognizing `es de` and `tiene` for another person;
- giving a short home or city fact.

## Language Patterns Introduced

- `mi`;
- family and friend words;
- `casa`, `ciudad`, `en`;
- third-person reuse of `es`, `tiene` and `es de`.

## Typical Learner Limitations

- Cannot yet describe family relationships broadly.
- Cannot narrate daily life.
- Needs profile-style input with known structures.

# Module 9 — Integrated A0 Communication

## Communicative Competencies

- Combine first-contact, origin, wellbeing, need and object language.
- Understand short controlled dialogues and profiles.
- Complete mixed recall and application tasks.
- Demonstrate the A0 foundation without relying on one memorized script.

## Typical Real-Life Scenarios

- meeting someone and exchanging basic personal information;
- asking for a basic item or help politely;
- reading a tiny integrated profile;
- handling controlled shopping, transport-help or health-need prompts when they reuse known language.

## Language Patterns Introduced

- no major new grammar;
- cumulative use of greetings, names, origin, wellbeing, numbers, `tener`, requests and simple profiles;
- checkpoint-style integration of reading, recall and application.

## Typical Learner Limitations

- Cannot yet handle natural-speed conversation.
- Cannot manage open-ended travel, shopping or health tasks.
- Can communicate only inside short, controlled A0 scenarios.

---

# Final Principle

Every module should expand what the learner can do in a realistic situation.

Grammar and vocabulary are included only when they serve that communicative growth.
