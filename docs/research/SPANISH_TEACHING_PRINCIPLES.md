# Spanish Teaching Principles

Status: R2E11 research artifact.

This document normalizes principles extracted from the local Spanish textbook
collection and classifies them for Tutor Language as `KEEP`, `ADAPT`, or
`DO NOT USE`.

No lesson content, textbook exercise, or textbook dialogue is reproduced here.

## Evidence Map

Abbreviations:

- DY: Dyshlevaya, `Курс испанского языка для начинающих`.
- RK: Raevskaya and Kovrigina, `Испанский язык: новый самоучитель`.
- RPS: Rodriguez-Danilevskaya, Patrushev, and Stepunina,
  `Учебник испанского языка. Практический курс для начинающих`.
- MA: Malinskaya, `Экспресс-самоучитель испанского языка`.

## Principles

### 1. Prepare Reading Before Unsupported Spanish

Evidence:
DY, RK, and MA provide an introductory pronunciation or phonetic course. RPS
starts with sounds and reading behavior inside the first lesson.

Classification:
KEEP.

Tutor Language rule:
The learner must not be asked to read or type an unfamiliar Spanish word before
the necessary pronunciation support has appeared.

### 2. Teach Only the Reading Fact Needed Now

Evidence:
Traditional books often present tables of many rules, but the exercises
immediately ask the learner to apply specific rules to words. The useful
pedagogical unit is the applied rule, not the table.

Classification:
ADAPT.

Tutor Language rule:
A reading rule should be introduced when it enables the next learner action. A
full reference catalogue belongs outside a beginner lesson flow.

### 3. Use Examples Before Abstraction

Evidence:
All readable sources pair rules with examples, and several ask learners to read
or repeat examples immediately after a rule.

Classification:
KEEP.

Tutor Language rule:
The app should show a concrete Spanish item and a learner-friendly explanation
before any abstract label. Linguistic terminology may appear only when it is the
lesson objective or a necessary support.

### 4. Recognition Comes Before Recall

Evidence:
Textbooks commonly move from listening, reading, or selecting to answering,
transforming, translating, or retelling.

Classification:
KEEP.

Tutor Language rule:
The scenario should normally progress through supported encounter, recognition,
guided recall, independent recall, and contextual use.

### 5. Repetition Must Change the Learner Action

Evidence:
Textbooks reuse sounds, words, and grammar through varied tasks: reading,
classification, question-answer work, translation, and retelling.

Classification:
KEEP.

Tutor Language rule:
Repeating the same visible item is justified only when support, context, response
type, retrieval demand, or communicative purpose changes.

### 6. Communication Motivates Technical Work

Evidence:
RK and MA connect early study to first-contact situations. Even phonetic-first
books later use reading and grammar to support communicative texts and dialogues.

Classification:
KEEP.

Tutor Language rule:
A beginner lesson should make the practical purpose visible quickly. The learner
should know what real action the word, sound, or pattern enables.

### 7. Large Textbook Lessons Are Not Mobile Lessons

Evidence:
RK frames lessons as multi-hour units. RPS expects large vocabulary and text
work. MA compresses many phrases and grammar points into one self-study unit.

Classification:
ADAPT.

Tutor Language rule:
The textbook lesson is a source of sequence patterns, not a unit-size model. A
Tutor Language A0 lesson should teach one measurable skill.

### 8. Teacher Choice Must Become Deterministic Logic

Evidence:
RK and RPS explicitly rely on teacher choice, teacher comments, oral modeling,
and optional exercise selection.

Classification:
ADAPT.

Tutor Language rule:
Any teacher-dependent decision must become a deterministic support rule,
feedback branch, or remediation path.

### 9. Heavy Early Grammar Load Should Not Be Copied

Evidence:
The textbooks often introduce articles, gender, number, pronouns, word order,
and high-frequency verbs very early.

Classification:
DO NOT USE as a mobile A0 lesson pattern.

Tutor Language rule:
Do not introduce a grammar category unless it directly helps the next learner
action. Prefer usable examples before naming a pattern.

### 10. Dictionary-Like Vocabulary Lists Should Not Drive Lessons

Evidence:
Traditional chapters often contain topic lists or extensive word sets. They are
supported by a teacher, long sessions, and later exercises.

Classification:
DO NOT USE as lesson design.

Tutor Language rule:
New vocabulary must appear as part of a measurable action, with pronunciation,
meaning, and immediate use.

## Reading Rule Findings

### Spanish Vowels

Textbook pattern:
Vowels are introduced early. Sources emphasize stable pronunciation and practice
through examples and reading.

Tutor Language adaptation:
Introduce vowel behavior only as needed for the next word. Give support-language
pronunciation first and test recognition before typing.

### Silent `h`

Textbook pattern:
The silent `h` is usually part of early reading tables and is practiced through
examples immediately.

Tutor Language adaptation:
Teach the rule only when an `h` word appears in the lesson path. The card should
answer how to read the word, not explain orthographic history.

### `ll`

Textbook pattern:
`ll` is treated as a special spelling pattern with a practical sound
approximation and examples.

Tutor Language adaptation:
Introduce `ll` as a reading aid for a concrete upcoming word. Avoid a full
dialect discussion unless the course objective requires it.

### `ñ`

Textbook pattern:
`ñ` receives early attention because it is visually distinctive and
pronunciation-critical.

Tutor Language adaptation:
Use a familiar support-language approximation, then examples. IPA may be shown
only after the learner explanation.

### Single `r` and `rr`

Textbook pattern:
Sources distinguish the short single sound from the stronger trill, usually with
position rules and examples.

Tutor Language adaptation:
Teach only the contrast required for the current word set. Do not introduce
articulatory terminology as the first explanation.

### `c` and `z`

Textbook pattern:
The books explain position-dependent reading and often include regional or
Castilian comments.

Tutor Language adaptation:
For beginner mobile lessons, teach the course pronunciation policy and postpone
regional comparison unless it is a stated objective.

### `g`, `j`, `gue`, and `gui`

Textbook pattern:
The books introduce these as position-dependent or spelling-dependent reading
rules and give examples.

Tutor Language adaptation:
Split these into separate micro-rules. Never combine too many spelling
conditions on one beginner screen.

### `qu`

Textbook pattern:
`qu` appears as a practical reading pattern for `que` and `qui`, with immediate
examples.

Tutor Language adaptation:
Introduce the pattern only before a word or phrase that needs it. The learner's
first task is reading the next Spanish item, not learning a spelling system.

### Stress and Intonation

Textbook pattern:
Stress and intonation are introduced early, often with reading aloud and
question patterns.

Tutor Language adaptation:
Use stress and intonation support when they affect the next learner action.
Avoid a full rule set before the learner has enough words to hear the value.

## Teacher Functions and App Replacement

| Teacher function | Textbook evidence | Tutor Language replacement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Motivation | First-contact themes and prefaces explain usefulness. | One-sentence purpose tied to the immediate learner action. |
| Pronunciation model | RPS and others expect audio, teacher repetition, or reading aloud. | Local pronunciation support, transcription, audio if available, and supported decoding. |
| Error anticipation | Reading sections warn about silent letters, position rules, and contrasts. | Micro-warning only before a predictable error, then immediate practice. |
| Correction | Classroom courses expect teacher correction. | Deterministic feedback tied to the failed learner state. |
| Pacing | Teachers choose optional exercises and timing. | Learning state machine controls support fading and remediation. |
| Transition | Teachers move from sound to word to sentence to use. | Scenario steps must state why each step exists and what changes in learner state. |
| Confidence building | Books use early simple phrases and repeated practice. | Small visible success, short tasks, and completion evidence. |
| Context creation | Dialogues and texts provide communicative situations. | Minimal realistic micro-context using only known or immediately introduced language. |

## KEEP

- Concrete examples before abstraction.
- Pronunciation support before unsupported reading.
- Recognition before recall.
- Guided recall before independent recall.
- Immediate practice after a new reading fact.
- Reuse of previous material in changed contexts.
- Communicative purpose for technical learning.
- Review after interference and delay.

## ADAPT

- Alphabet tables become just-in-time reading support.
- Multi-hour textbook lessons become micro-lessons with one measurable skill.
- Teacher-selected optional exercises become deterministic adaptive branches.
- Long grammar explanations become example-first pattern support.
- Large dialogues become minimal communication scenes with known language.
- Translation drills become controlled meaning and recall tasks.

## DO NOT USE

- Full phonetic catalogues as the first learner experience.
- Large first-lesson vocabulary loads.
- Many grammar categories in one beginner lesson.
- Dictionary entries as lesson structure.
- Teacher-dependent open tasks without app-checkable evidence.
- Technical terminology as the first explanation.
- Exercises that repeat the same item without changing learner demand.

## Derived Design Constraint

Every beginner activity must answer one of these questions:

- What should the learner notice?
- What should the learner understand?
- What should the learner read with support?
- What should the learner recognize without full support?
- What should the learner recall?
- What should the learner use in context?
- What failure should the app remediate?

If an activity cannot answer one of these questions, it is not a beginner lesson
step.
